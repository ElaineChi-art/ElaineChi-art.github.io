The House Method

Slow is the only method.

A perfume can be assembled in an afternoon. Ours cannot. The difference is not romance — it is the time raw materials need to stop arguing with one another.

Hand-Blown Crystal

Each flacon is gathered, blown and cut by a single glassworker in Normandy. The walls are deliberately thick, so the juice inside is magnified rather than merely contained.

Six-Month Maceration

The concentrate rests in stainless steel at fourteen degrees. Nothing is added to accelerate it. What emerges is rounder, quieter, and impossible to fake.

Oud, Not Oud Accord

Real agarwood from a single Assam plantation, at a dosage most houses would consider commercially irresponsible. It is the reason the drydown lasts eleven hours.

Turned Brass Collar

Cut from solid bar stock, knurled by hand, then gilded. It threads onto the neck with a quarter turn and holds a pressure seal without a gasket.

Forty-One Materials

Twenty-nine naturals, twelve molecules. The formula is published on the carton in full — an unusual decision, and one we have never regretted.

Refill, Do Not Replace

The flacon is designed to outlive the fragrance inside it. Return it to any boutique and it is cleaned, resealed and refilled at a third of the price.
