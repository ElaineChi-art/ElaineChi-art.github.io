Fewer Parts, Better Parts

A running shoe is mostly glue.

A typical performance runner is thirty-odd pieces bonded together, which is why it cannot be repaired and will not be recycled. Course 01 is six, and every joint is mechanical.

Seamless Knit

The upper leaves the machine as one tube. No cut panels, no stitching, no offcuts — which also means no seam to abrade against the fifth metatarsal.

Supercritical Foam

Nitrogen forced into the polymer under pressure, then released. The cells come out uniform, which is why the rebound is still 78 % after six hundred kilometres.

Replaceable Outsole

Held on by a mechanical lap joint, not adhesive. Send the shoe back, we press a new outsole on and return it. Four times, then the foam is done anyway.

Floating Lace Cage

The eyelets sit on a webbing cradle that is anchored to the midsole, not the knit. Pull the laces and the shoe tightens around the foot instead of crushing it.

Moulded Heel Counter

A thermoplastic cup laminated inside the knit, so the heel is held without a stiff external clip. It is the only part of the shoe that is not soft to the touch.

Six Hundred Kilometres

Not a marketing figure. Every batch is run to failure on a mechanical gait rig, and the number we print is the tenth percentile, not the average.
